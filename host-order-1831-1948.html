Under the foreclosed-alternatives ledger and the conjunction model sits a substrate — the strength of the host order itself — and this ledger dates its instruments: the emancipation edicts, the Land Code, the foreign-ownership law, the consular enforcement layer that made them real, the caps that tried to close what the reforms had opened, and the two imperial collapses that ended the question. The consular-capitulatory layer: the courts the reforms didn't supply. Withdrawn 1914; buried at Lausanne.

The before-and-after in one detail: Montefiore's Jaffa orchard (1855) and Mishkenot Sha'ananim (1860) each required a personal imperial firman — a sultan's individual dispensation per transaction. Two decades after the stack completed, purchase was routine registration. And the Land Code did one more thing, perversely decisive: fellahin under-registered to dodge taxes and conscription while notables registered en bloc, so a cash-starved state selling whole village clusters (the Sursuq purchases, 1869/1872 — 230,000 dunums for £20,000) manufactured not only the buyer's legal capacity but the seller class whose consolidated tracts made purchase-at-scale possible.

The double edge. The same enfeeblement that made the reforms unrefusable made the empire fear Jewish concentration: having watched Greece, Serbia, Bulgaria and the Armenian question run the European-patronized-minority template, the Porte read Zionism as the same play — which is why the 1881–82 restrictions targeted concentration in Palestine while welcoming Jews elsewhere in the empire. Band-width tracked imperial confidence: the strong sixteenth-century empire absorbed the entire Sephardi expulsion; the weak nineteenth-century one rationed a few thousand entries at Jaffa. And the weakness that motivated the cap made it unenforceable — the state whose finances passed to the European-run Public Debt Administration in 1881 was attempting demographic sovereignty over one district, and on the order of 60,000 Jews entered against standing policy by 1914.

Same instrument, opposite outcome. A sovereign's cap on Jewish entry appears twice in this file. The Hamidian cap fell on ~25,000 subsidized dependents and yielded leakage and compliance. The 1939 cap fell on ~475,000 people with a para-state, a trained officer cadre, and a fleet-improvising Mossad le-Aliyah Bet — and yielded Lehi, the blockade war, and the insurgency that helped exhaust the patron. Suppression before the tracks matured caps the movement; suppression after merely selects its enemies.

Guardrails (symmetric rigor). The unsealing was not pro-Jewish design: it was general capitulation-weakness, and Christian protégés and Templer colonists were earlier beneficiaries of the same layer. The consular shield that protected Jews was the same battering ram that gutted Ottoman sovereignty — and much of the majority experienced the equality edicts themselves as a violation of the established order (Rose's 1841 dispatch; Damascus 1860; Lewis on the dhimma). The reforms were extracted under duress, not believed. And the British successor ran the same arc in reverse — charter, zigzag, cap, navy, exhaustion, withdrawal — its 1940 land freeze echoing the 1892 Hamidian one, its 1947 fiscal collapse echoing 1875–81. The removal test closes the file: no Ottoman collapse → the cap holds, the Yishuv plateaus as a capped millet, no charter ever exists — and after 1924, European Jewry faces the 1930s with no exit at all.

How to read this ledger

Six instrument lanes. Status (who may hold and litigate), tenure (what may be owned and sold), entry (who may come), enforcement (what makes any of it real), strength (the sovereign's own capacity), and sovereignty (who holds the ground at all).

Effects are graded, not narrated. unseals opens ground; seals closes it; enforces makes parchment real; leaks marks a sovereign's failure to hold its own line; capacity indexes the substrate's strength; transfers changes the sovereign. The story of the page is that every seal by a weak sovereign leaked, and both sovereigns exited without successors.

Division of labor. This is the sovereign-side file. What the closures did to the alternatives is the foreclosed-alternatives ledger; what the land instruments did parcel by parcel is the land-regime page; who came through the doors is the in-migration ledger set. Rows cross-reference rather than duplicate.

Commission and consular documents are not transparent fact. The Survey, the Cmd. papers, and the FO files are quoted as records of what the sovereign found, decreed, and admitted about itself — their framings are read against the corpus, per project discipline.
