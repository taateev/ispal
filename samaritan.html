An indigenous Palestinian religious community ground to the edge of extinction — from ~1,400 in the 16th-century tax registers to a single endogamous remnant of ~140 at Nablus by 1900, its historic nadir, before a slow recovery. The dominant cause was religious coercion — forced conversion under the apostasy ratchet, central Gerizim bans, the 1841 "convert-or-exterminate" ruling — with fiscal extortion and the tiny gene pool as aggravators. Population & the documented record, by year

Collapse, then a long remnant plateau. The community fell an order of magnitude across ~1.5 centuries — mostly the dissolution of the Gaza, Cairo and Damascus diaspora — then held at ~140 souls at Nablus alone. The one window of relief was the 1831–40 Egyptian decade; the 1839–58 Tanzimat, with no great-power patron behind it, barely touched them.
