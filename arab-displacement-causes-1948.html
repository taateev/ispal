Corpus synthesis · 1947–1949

Causes of the 1948 Palestinian displacement

Headline · there is no honest single “% expelled vs. % fled”
Why · the exodus was phased and multi-causal; the anchor source refuses to aggregate
Benny Morris, Birth of the Palestinian Refugee Problem Revisited (2004)

Roughly 700,000 Palestinian Arabs became refugees in 1947–49. The natural question — what share were expelled, what share fled the fighting, what share left on their own? — has no clean numerical answer, and the reason is itself the finding. The most thorough archival source, Benny Morris, codes the cause of departure village by village but deliberately never adds those codes into population percentages, because for most places a single cause cannot be isolated. What the corpus can give is a phased picture, a documented floor and ceiling for outright expulsion, and a split between places where staying was urged and places where it was not. For the village-level data beneath this summary — 378 datable events, each tagged with Morris's own cause-code — see the 1948 depopulation ledger.

No percentage is honest at the decimal. Morris's village-level map (Map 2 of Birth Revisited) assigns each depopulated locality one of six cause-codes, but he states flatly that the lines between them “are somewhat blurred” and that “a single-cause explanation of the exodus from most sites is untenable.” He never merges the codings into a population-by-cause total. So any source offering a tidy “X% expelled, Y% fled” pie is manufacturing precision the archive withholds. The figures below are magnitudes and ranges, not a partition that sums to 100%.

Morris's six cause-codes

The village-level vocabulary — what each departure was coded as, not how many people each accounts for.

M
Military assault on the locality
The single most common code — flight under or just ahead of an attack on the town or village itself.
E
Expulsion by Jewish forces
Inhabitants ordered out — the category with the firmest documentary floor (Lydda–Ramle).
C
Influence of a nearby town's fall
Departure triggered by the collapse of a neighbouring centre, without a direct attack.
F
Fear / general insecurity
Pre-emptive flight in anticipation of attack — no order, no battle at the point of departure.
W
Whispering-campaign psychological warfare
Flight accelerated by deliberate rumour and the exploitation of atrocity-fear (e.g. Deir Yassin).
A
Abandonment on Arab orders
Local Arab commanders / committees ordering specific evacuations — real but never a blanket national order.

Morris: “the lines between C, F and M are somewhat blurred” — which is exactly why a population partition cannot be built from the codes.

The phased picture · Morris's four waves

The closest thing to a breakdown by cause: each wave has a dominant mechanism and an approximate magnitude. Figures are cumulative through the phase, not a clean partition.

WavePeriodApprox. sizeDominant cause Morris attributes
First waveDec 1947 – Mar 1948~100,000Self-evacuation of the urban upper and middle classes (Jaffa, Haifa, Jerusalem) under civil-war conditions. The AHC and National Committees were trying to prevent this departure.
Second waveApr – Jun 1948250,000–300,000Jewish military assault. Morris dates a spring-1948 shift to “a primacy of external, compulsive causes: Haganah/IDF attacks and expulsions, fear of Jewish attacks and atrocities.” Plan D + the falls of Haifa, Jaffa, Tiberias, Safad. No blanket Arab evacuation order in the archive — but the assaults routinely included fire into civilian quarters to induce flight (below).
Third waveJul – Oct 1948~100,000+The “Ten Days” + Second Truce. Includes Lydda–Ramle, the single largest direct expulsion, carried out under a written order. Operations Dani, Dekel, Brosh.
Fourth waveOct – Nov 1948substantialOperations Yoav (south) and Hiram (Galilee). Allon: “almost no Arab civilians remained.” Hiram mixed sharply by community — Druze and Christian largely retained, Muslim villages expelled or fled, with heavy village-level variation.
Border-clearing tailNov 1948 – 195020,000–40,000Clearing a 5–7 km strip along the Lebanese border; anti-infiltration sweeps; specific village expulsions (Iqrit, Bir'im — promised return, never permitted).

Read as magnitudes, not a partition. The single largest slice is the second wave's flight under military assault (~40% of the total); urban self-evacuation opens the sequence (~15%); the documented hard core of outright expulsion is concentrated in the third and fourth waves. Total working figure ~700,000 (Morris: “~700,000 is probably a fair estimate”).

What “military assault” actually means

The second-wave attribution is read two over-clean ways — pure combat with civilians fleeing incidentally, or pre-planned cleansing from the start. The record sits between, and the attackers put part of it in writing.

Jaffa · the stated objective of the IZL barrage
The aim of the three-day, 20-ton mortar barrage was “to break the spirit of the enemy troops, [and] to cause chaos among the civilian population in order to create a mass flight.”
— IZL operations chief Amihai Paglin's pre-attack briefing, 25–27 April 1948. High Commissioner Cunningham cabled that the attack was “indiscriminate and designed to create panic among the civilian inhabitants.” Per Haganah intelligence, most casualties were civilians. Via Morris, Birth Revisited, pp. 212–214.
The synthesis. The bulk of the ~250–300k fled under or ahead of military assault on their own towns — but that assault routinely included deliberate fire onto civilian-populated areas and the exploitation of atrocity-fear (Deir Yassin, code W) precisely to induce flight. “Against combatants only” erases the part the attackers wrote down; “cleansing from the start” reads the post-July policy back onto April–June, when no national expulsion policy was yet in force. Jaffa is the cleanest documented case of operational intent to empty a civilian area.

Direct expulsion · a floor and a ceiling, not a point

Where the corpus can be quantitative: the share attributable to outright expulsion orders runs from a hard documented floor to a broad-reading ceiling.

Hard floor
~70,000
Lydda–Ramle alone — the one case with both a written order at operational-HQ level and a clean population figure.
Defensible mid-range
~150,000–200,000
Adds the Ten Days / Operation Hiram villages Morris attributes to “expulsive guidelines,” plus the 1948–50 border-clearing.
Broad-reading ceiling
~700,000
Morris's late framing: all who ended as refugees, when “expulsion” is read to include barred return after flight.
Lydda · the written expulsion order, 12 July 1948
“The inhabitants of Lydda must be expelled quickly without attention to age.”
— Operation Dani HQ to Yiftah Brigade, written order timed 13:30, 12 July 1948 (two archival copies, IDFA). The largest single expulsion of the war; ~50,000–70,000 from Lydda and Ramle together. Via Morris, Birth Revisited.

The gap between the ~70k floor and the ~700k ceiling is the interpretive cleavage between the Morris-archival reading and the Pappé/Masalha “predetermined cleansing” reading. We apply a standing selection-bias caveat to the upper end (it under-engages the retention evidence below) and to the older British-eyewitness lower end (it over-credits an unrecovered Arab radio “evacuation broadcast”).

The other half of the question · where staying was urged

Expulsion and flight are only one side. In a large minority of places Jewish authorities actively urged Arabs to stay or refrained from expelling them — with very different results by city and community.

The aggregate split (a corpus aggregation, not a Morris number). Pooling the urban centres, the Druze and Christian villages, the strategic-axis exceptions, and the off-operational-path Muslim Galilee villages: roughly 40% of the pre-war Arab population was in a category where Jewish authorities urged or allowed staying (retention worked for about half of them); the other ~60% was in conditions where retention was never on offer — the rural south, the Lydda–Ramle corridor, the coastal-plain villages, the Galilee Muslim villages in the operational path, and the borderland clearances. About 150,000 Arabs remained in Israel at war's end.

In the primary & secondary record

The cited pages of Morris's Birth Revisited, as printed — an expulsion order, a retention appeal, and the conclusion's own multi-causal thesis. Read them side by side: the war ran both ways.

Symmetric caveats

Both sides opposed the flight in the first wave. Dec 1947 – Mar 1948, the AHC and National Committees actively tried to prevent civilian departure — border closures, exit-visa restrictions, taxes on émigré families, an 8 Jan 1948 proclamation denying it had ordered evacuations. The early exodus was opposed by both leaderships.

No blanket Arab evacuation order — but local ones existed. Morris's archival finding is that there was no AHC general radio broadcast or blanket order (the older “the Arabs told them to leave” framing rests on a broadcast text never recovered). But local Arab commanders did order specific evacuations, and committees approved removing women, children and the elderly from front-line villages — coded A.

The retained population skews Christian and Druze, and the timing inverts. A disproportionate share of those who stayed were Christian or Druze; the IDF “related far more benignly to Christians and Druse than to Muslims” (Morris). And the retention efforts characterise the first half of the war (Haifa, Acre, Tiberias) while active expulsion characterises the second (Lydda–Ramle, Hiram, the border clearances) — the policy ran one way, then the other.

Sources. Benny Morris, The Birth of the Palestinian Refugee Problem Revisited (Cambridge UP, 2004) — the load-bearing archival anchor — read against Walid Khalidi, All That Remains; Yoav Gelber, Palestine 1948; and Hillel Cohen, Army of Shadows. All in the project corpus.

Scope & rigor. The percentages here are corpus aggregations across the cases Morris documents, not figures Morris asserts; he declines to merge his village cause-codes into population totals, and that refusal is reported rather than papered over. The same evidentiary standard is applied to both sides: documented Jewish expulsion orders and documented Jewish retention appeals are both recorded; documented Arab anti-flight measures and documented local Arab evacuation orders are both recorded. Full working synthesis (totals, every expulsion-order document, the realized-returns and 1949 repatriation-offer record) in the research repository — topic.1948_refugee_causation.
