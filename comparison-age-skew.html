In Gaza, 74% of the adult dead are male (35,766 of 48,211) — the military-age-male concentration that lifts the adult rate above the child rate. In the Holocaust the order flips: adults died at 1 in 1.5, children at 1 in 1.06. Chechen and Tutsi splits are soft (wide kill ranges, unstated child cutoffs). Gaza figures are identified deaths; independent studies suggest a ~35–41% undercount, weighted toward adult males under rubble.

Comparative Data · The age-skew of who died

Was a child or an adult more likely to be killed?

The odds ratio of a child’s death versus an adult’s, within each population. Below 1, children were relatively spared; above 1, children were killed at higher odds. The odds ratio — not relative risk — is used because at near-total death rates it alone captures how completely child survival collapsed.

◗ left = a war’s pattern (fighting-age adults die, children survive)  ·  right = an extermination’s pattern (child survival driven toward zero)

This measures the age-pattern of lethality — it is not a legal determination of genocide. Genocide turns on intent to destroy a group, which is multi-factorial; an overwhelmingly adult-male killing can still be genocide (Srebrenica, ~8,000 men and boys). A collapsed child-survival ratio is a strong demographic signature of an eliminationist pattern, not proof of one — and its absence does not rule one out. Data: Gaza firm (identified register, ~35–41% undercount); Holocaust firm-ish (USHMM 6–11% child vs 33% adult survival); Chechnya & Rwanda soft (illustrative).
