A widely-shared graphic pairs two pie charts: of the UN's country resolutions, more than half name Israel — yet the Israeli–Palestinian conflict is a fraction of a percent of the world's conflict deaths. This page asks the same question of news and public attention, across 22 conflicts, computed from public data — over 2015–2023, a nine-year window chosen so the 2024 Oct-7 coverage peak is not doing the work. It takes no position on whether any level of attention is deserved; it measures the gap and shows the arithmetic.

Three things are measured for each conflict: how much the public looked it up (2015–2023 English-Wikipedia pageviews of the conflict's umbrella article), how much the world's institutions condemned it (UN resolutions), and how many it has killed (cumulative deaths, best available count). The method is uniform — the same window and rule for every conflict — and reproducible. Thailand's South-Thailand insurgency is the control: a real, deadly, twenty-year war that draws almost no global attention. The headline survives both dropping the 2024 spike and widening the field to 22 conflicts: over 2015–2023, Israel / Palestine is the most over-attended conflict that has a substantial death toll — while the era's deadliest neglected wars (DR Congo, Colombia, South Sudan, Mexico) draw a fraction of the attention. (The Russia–Ukraine war drew the most attention in absolute terms; a handful of tiny-casualty conflicts post higher raw ratios and are flagged on the charts.)

How the numbers are made.  Public attention = total 2015–2023 English-Wikipedia pageviews of each conflict's longest-standing umbrella article (Wikimedia per-article/monthly REST API; data begins Jul 2015). Umbrella — not acute-war — articles are used because the acute articles (e.g. Israel–Hamas war, Russian invasion of Ukraine) did not exist for most of the window.  News coverage = article counts in The Guardian (a single major outlet, editorial country tags, complete 2015–2023) — an independent cross-check on the public measure (§5).  Institutional attention = UN General Assembly country resolutions 2015–2023 (UN Watch tabulation — an advocacy NGO, but the raw counts are verifiable against the UN record).  Severity = cumulative deaths of each conflict's matching phase, source-tagged with window + basis; comparator tolls are chosen conservatively against the over-attention finding.  Press volume = GDELT global news volume (§10); its index begins 2017, so that panel is 2017–2023, and GDELT's per-IP cap left it partial. Scripts: attention-analysis/ in the research repo.

The institutional disparity is the largest of the three — and the subject of §2.

2 · The institutional disparity — Israel at the UN

Of the three denominators, the institutional one is the most lopsided — and the most verifiable, because much of it is not a count at all but a fixture written into the UN’s own rulebook. Israel is the only country the UN system singles out by name across this many standing mechanisms:

Only country with
A permanent agenda item
Item 7 — ‘the human rights situation in Palestine and other occupied Arab territories’ — is the only country-specific standing item on the Human Rights Council’s 10-item agenda, debated at every session. Every other country on earth is handled under the generic Item 4.
Only country with
An open-ended Commission of Inquiry
The Commission of Inquiry established in May 2021 is the first in the Council’s history with no end date — a standing, annually-funded investigation. Every prior COI (Syria, Myanmar, …) was time-limited.
Only country with
A no-expiry Special Rapporteur
The Special Rapporteur ‘on… the Palestinian territories occupied since 1967’ (since 1993) is the only country mandate with no periodic review or end date built in.
Only country with
The most emergency sessions
9 of the Council’s ~32 country-specific special sessions have addressed Israel — more than Syria (5), Myanmar (3) or any other state. North Korea: 1.
Only country with
A women’s-rights condemnation
The Commission on the Status of Women names exactly one country in its annual country-specific resolution: Israel. Not Iran, Saudi Arabia or Afghanistan.
Only country with
A health condemnation
The World Health Assembly’s only country-specific resolution, adopted annually, is on ‘health conditions in the occupied Palestinian territory’. No other country is named — not Syria, Yemen or Sudan.
Only country with
A dedicated standing apparatus
A permanent committee (CEIRPP, 1975), a Secretariat division (Division for Palestinian Rights), a dedicated refugee agency (UNRWA — vs UNHCR for every other refugee), and an annual International Day of Solidarity (29 Nov): an institutional infrastructure no other people has.

Israel drew 108 Human Rights Council resolutions — 54% of all those passed against these eight most-condemned states, and more than the next four combined. At the General Assembly, 2015–2023: 154 resolutions on Israel vs 71 on every other state on earth combined. Against Israel’s 1.2% share of these conflicts’ deaths (§1), this is a far larger gap than the news or public-attention measures show — the institutional disparity is the biggest of the three.

How to read this — sources, both interpretations, and the gaps

What is fact vs. what is a count. The “only country with” items are structural facts, verifiable from the UN’s own records — the Council’s published 10-item agenda (Item 7), the OHCHR mandate pages (the open-ended Commission of Inquiry and Special Rapporteur), and the World Health Assembly / Commission on the Status of Women resolution texts. The resolution tallies come from the UN Watch database — a pro-Israel advocacy NGO, flagged as such; its raw counts are checkable against the UN Official Document System, but they are one organization’s tabulation, not an audited census.

The same numbers carry two readings. One side (UN Watch, Israel, the US) calls this structural bias — a body that condemns one democracy more than the rest of the world combined while passing nothing on China or zero on most active wars. The other (the Palestinians, much of the Global South, many human-rights organizations) calls it appropriate sustained accountability for a belligerent occupation the UN and the ICJ deem unlawful and ongoing — a continuing situation that, unlike a discrete war, invites continuing scrutiny. This page documents that the disparity is real and largely structural; it does not adjudicate which reading is right.

Why the UN focuses here at all (the unmeasured context). Israel is a UN member militarily controlling territory the UN considers occupied; the “Question of Palestine” has been a standing UN file since the 1947 partition plan, with the General Assembly asserting a “permanent responsibility until the question is resolved.” Much of the apparatus dates to that mandate and to Global-South / Non-Aligned bloc majorities — politics this page measures the output of, not the merits of.

What we don’t have. There is no single audited cross-body per-country ledger of UN attention; the counts lean on one advocacy source; budgets per mechanism aren’t compiled here; and a like-for-like structural comparison with the world’s other long occupations and frozen conflicts (Western Sahara, Cyprus, Kashmir, Tibet) — which have no comparable standing UN machinery — is asserted from the absence of such bodies, not from a line-by-line audit. The structural “onlys” are firm; the completeness of the comparison is not.

3 · Attention vs. deaths — the whole field at once

Each conflict plotted by how many it has killed (horizontal) against its 2015–2023 public attention (vertical), both on log scales. The dashed line is proportional attention — where a conflict would sit if attention tracked deaths exactly. Above the line is over-attended; below, under-attended.

Israel / Palestine sits far above the line — high attention, mid-field deaths. Russia / Ukraine drew the most attention of all, but its death toll is large too, so it sits closer to the line. DR Congo, Ethiopia (Tigray), Syria and Darfur sit below it: among the deadliest wars of the era, with a fraction of the lookups. Thailand (the control) sits low-and-left — almost invisible in absolute terms.

7 · What pinning to 2024 would do — the recency spike

2024 was the single most intense year of Israeli–Palestinian coverage on record. Re-running the identical computation on 2024 alone (using each conflict's acute-war article) shows what the window choice is worth — and why this page does not pin to it.

Israel / Palestine · share of attention
16% → 39%
2015–2023 → 2024. The "Israel–Hamas war" article alone drew 12.0M views in 2024 — the most of any conflict that year, ahead of the Russian invasion (9.3M) and the Syrian civil war (5.6M).
Israel / Palestine · vs. proportional
13.3× → 31.9×
Pinning to 2024 nearly doubles the over-attention factor and lifts Israel / Palestine from second to first in raw attention. The 2015–2023 window is the conservative, disinterested choice; the disproportion is present in both.

9 · The confounds — where this measurement is weak

Read these before drawing a conclusion

Wikipedia titles drift — these are ±~15%. Conflicts get renamed and split across articles; the figures use each conflict's longest-standing umbrella title (Yemen's article alone was renamed three times). The order-of-magnitude comparison is robust to this; a single row is not precise to the last digit.

Deaths are a stock; attention is a 2015–2023 flow. Comparing a cumulative death toll to a nine-year attention window flatters conflicts acute within it and penalizes older or frozen ones. Because the I/P attention here excludes the 2024 peak while its death toll still counts 2024, the Israel figure is if anything conservative (see §7).

The denominator is 22 conflicts, not "the world." The pie shares are across the conflicts on this page. The original graphic's "fraction of a percent of world conflict deaths" used the global total, which is smaller still; either way the direction is the same.

"Deaths" is the wrong axis for some cases. China / Xinjiang is mass detention, not a body count — it has zero UN Human Rights Council resolutions and would not appear on a deaths chart, yet is a severe-repression case. UAE and Qatar have no domestic conflict. The death metric systematically misses repression that does not kill at scale.

Umbrella ≠ acute war. Using whole-conflict articles (e.g. War in Darfur for Sudan rather than the 2023 RSF–SAF war) captures the attention visible across the window but can miss a recent catastrophe — Sudan's 2023 war is, if anything, even more under-covered relative to its toll.

And the comparison itself is a known move. "Why focus on Israel when X is worse" is a recognizable rhetorical device (advantageous comparison / whataboutism) — its rhetorical use is independent of whether the disproportion is real. This page documents that the disproportion is real and measurable; it does not claim any individual conflict's coverage is therefore unjustified. Legitimate drivers of elevated attention — a decades-long occupation, heavy US involvement, religious significance to three faiths, press access, and a democracy held to its own stated standards — are real and are not measured here.
