Across four centuries the Jewish communities of Palestine were repeatedly targeted as Jews — and the least-Zionist among them, the Old Yishuv of Hebron and Safed, died most. This ledger enumerates the 85 events behind one finding: the defense predicament was decoupled from Zionist political affiliation at the moment of violence, and welded to it in consequence — “in 1929 the Arabs created the Yishuv” (Cohen). The refined thesis. (1) Jewish soft-target vulnerability predates Zionism — via dhimmi physical defenselessness, not a pre-existing categorical animus. (2) In 1920 and 1929 victim-selection ran inverse to affiliation: the anti-Zionist Old Yishuv of Hebron rejected Haganah protection and was the single largest victim group. (3) That decoupling is exactly what welded the communities — the predicament drove non-Zionist Jews into organized defense.

Guardrail (symmetric rigor). “Qua Jews” is not “decoupled from Zionism”: Cohen’s own thesis is that 1929 fused all Jews to the Zionist project in Arab perception. Victim-decoupling is escalation-conditioned, not a stable structural fact; the mechanism (mob + accessibility) is not the same as intent; and protection tracked the power configuration too (the Druze rescued Safed’s Jews in 1834, attacked them in 1838). The 18 counter-current rows carry these cases.

How to read this ledger

Five thesis lanes. Vulnerability (the pre-Zionist soft-target record, 1517–1846 — the baseline, not a categorical animus), decoupling (victims not sorted by Zionism), response (defense triggered by violence), welding (non-Zionists absorbed into defense), and counter-current (where the distinction held, or the clean reading is too strong). The Victims column marks each event’s targeted population by affiliation; Supports tags the lane; the detail panel carries the mechanism and the adversarial-verification verdict.

Vulnerability ≠ decoupling. The 30 pre-1882 rows are tagged Vulnerability, not decoupling — they cannot be “decoupled” from a movement that did not yet exist. They establish that the soft-target predicament (dhimmi defenselessness at every breakdown of order) long preceded Zionism; the categorical “qua-Jews” mob form is a later, 1929 development.

The reverse arrow is the point. The best-supported finding is not that violence ignored Zionism but that the decoupled predicament produced coupling: pre-1929 Sephardi participation in the Haganah was ~nil (“There were no members of the Sephardi community in the Haganah” — Yekutieli), and the riots drove Mizrahi and Old-Yishuv Jews into organized defense. Decoupled at the moment of violence; welded in the aftermath.

Symmetric rigor. Counter-current rows are retained, not buried (18 of 85): Arab neighbours who sheltered ~250 Jews at Hebron; the leadership/press distinction between Jews and Zionists (Antonius, Sakakini); the Jewish-side categorical violence (Irgun 1937–38 market bombings; Sadeh’s Haifa incursions). Commission framings (Shaw) are not read as transparent fact.
