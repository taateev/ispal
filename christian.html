A growth curve, not a collapse — the inverse of the Samaritan trajectory. The dashed pre-1847 segment is an estimate (Lewis & Cohen establish Christians as the largest minority from the 16th c., but give no single aggregate); the solid points are documented. Scope caveat: the 1847 figure is the Jerusalem pachalik only (a lower bound), while 1870s+ are all-Palestine, so the true 1847→1870s rise is gentler than the line implies. The 1839 Tanzimat line opens the era of great-power patronage that Christians — already rooted — exploited most.

Reading the record. Three registers are kept distinct: institutional/demographic growth (the metropole effect), the backlash it provoked (1840, 1860 — note the 1860 massacre was Damascus/Lebanon-centered, not Palestine), and the nationalist afterlife of the Arab-Orthodox laity. Anti-Zionism here is secular-nationalist; the European blood-libel repertoire (Damascus 1840) was Latin/French-channeled, with the Ottoman-Muslim state protecting the Jews — these are different things and not collapsed.
