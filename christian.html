Population & the documented record, by year

A growth curve, not a collapse — the inverse of the Samaritan trajectory. A rooted majority-minority from the 16th century, the community rose roughly fourfold across the Tanzimat as French, Russian and British patronage compounded — at the recurring cost of backlash (1840, 1860).

The dashed pre-1847 curve is an estimate (no single 16th–early-19th-c. aggregate survives); solid points are documented. Scope caveat: the 1847 figure is the Jerusalem pachalik only, while 1870s+ are all-Palestine, so the true rise is gentler than the line implies.

Reading the record. Three registers are kept distinct: institutional/demographic growth (the metropole effect), the backlash it provoked (1840, 1860 — note the 1860 massacre was Damascus/Lebanon-centered, not Palestine), and the nationalist afterlife of the Arab-Orthodox laity. Anti-Zionism here is secular-nationalist; the European blood-libel repertoire (Damascus 1840) was Latin/French-channeled, with the Ottoman-Muslim state protecting the Jews — these are different things and not collapsed.
