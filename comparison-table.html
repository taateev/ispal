*Where sources give a range, the table shows the midpoint; the full range and underlying sources appear in the bibliography below. Share of children killed = children killed ÷ child population, so the figure is comparable across cases regardless of how large each child population was. The Palestinian child population is the river-to-sea total (~40% under 18, ~2.97M), not Gaza's higher 47% share applied to everyone.

†The Palestinian child count is a named enumeration, not an estimate. 20,633 is the exact under-18 count from the Gaza MoH identified-fatalities roster (“List 12,” 68,844 names, cutoff 10 Nov 2025) — every record carries an age. Re-cut by threshold: under-15 = 16,243 · under-16 = 17,594 · under-18 = 20,633. Adjusting for the ~35–41% undercount of the official list raises the under-18 figure to ~31,900; the named 20,633 is the firm floor. The earlier ~18,600 (PCBS) sat below an actual under-18 count of the names. At 20,633 ÷ ~2.97M the river-to-sea under-18 share is 1 in 144 (Gaza-only: ~1 in 50).

The four columns are not measured on a common basis — read the children rows with three asymmetries in mind. (1) Age cutoff: only the Palestinian count is register-cuttable to a fixed line (under-18); the Chechen denominator is under-15, the Holocaust cutoff is undefined, the Tutsi figure mixes definitions. (2) Numerator basis: the table pairs the Holocaust’s demographic-maximum estimate (~1.5M) with Gaza’s documented-minimum named count (20,633) — symmetric treatment would put both at named-count or both at demographic-estimate level. (3) Population matching: a per-capita rate is only coherent when numerator and denominator describe the same people (Tutsi-killed ÷ Tutsi-population, not all-victims ÷ Tutsi). None of these is an arithmetic error; each is a basis choice that shapes the cross-case comparison.

All figures are contested and methods differ — direct killings, indirect/excess deaths, and definitions of "victim" vary by source. The table uses midpoints of the ranges above; choosing different bounds shifts the per-capita rates but not their relative ordering.

Variance. None of these are sample surveys with standard errors; the uncertainty is structural (register coverage, projection assumptions, wartime disruption). The Israeli register is firmest (±1–2%); the PCBS West Bank projection is the softest input, with a fringe critique (Ettinger) claiming up to ~1.75M overstatement — rejected by mainstream demographers but flagged here as the downside scenario. The summed Palestinian child figure is solid to ~±5–8%, skewed downward. For Gaza specifically, independent studies suggest the official death count is undercounted by ~35–41%, so the killed figures here are conservative.

Definition of "child." The age cutoff is not uniform — only the Palestinian figure is register-based: under 18, per the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (PCBS / Gaza MoH). The other three are demographic reconstructions with no common boundary: the Jewish-child toll is itself a range (USHMM: "more than 1 million and perhaps as many as 1.5 million," with under-12s and 13–18 "adolescents" treated differently), and the Rwandan ~300,000 and Chechen ~20,000 child tolls carry no stated cutoff. Because a higher boundary enlarges both numerator and denominator, the children-killed rates are only loosely comparable — treat the Palestinian rate as the most precisely defined and the Holocaust child rate as a wide band (~1 in 1.1 to 1 in 1.6).

Palestinian citizens of Israel experienced minimal direct killing but are included in the population denominator as people under IDF jurisdiction. East Jerusalem (~360–400K) is counted once. Gaza/West Bank totals are for an ongoing event (through Jan 2026) and continue to change.
