Gaza casualties: the Ministry of Health data

What the named-fatality lists actually count — across twelve releases, the three intake methodologies, and why a recovered body is not required for a death to be listed.

Compiled 2026-06-07 · Sources: Gaza MoH list releases; Epstein, Policy Note 158 (Washington Institute, May 2025); AOAV; Drop Site News; Tech for Palestine; this project’s named-fatality roster. Full analysis in gaza-moh-list12-comparative-casualty-analysis.md.

Master caveat. The MoH lists name identified individuals only — a subset of the total reported toll, and well below capture–recapture estimates (Lancet 2025: ~64,260 traumatic deaths to 30 Jun 2024, 95% CI 55,298–78,525, ~41% above the official count). To be on the list a person must be identified and determined dead; a recovered body is one route, a judicially-verified family report is the other. People merely missing — including the Government Media Office's separately-claimed ~10,000–14,000 under the rubble — are not in the named list. Figures here are neutral data provenance, not a casualty estimate.

MethodologyHow the count is built — and what it leaves out

Three intake methodologies. Per Epstein's Policy Note 158, the toll is assembled from (1) the hospital / morgue central-collection system; (2) relative reports via an online portal live since January 2024 — added unvetted at first, but routed through a judicial committee since October 2024; and (3) a “media reports” / “unidentified” category used heavily Nov 2023 – Jul 2024 and drawn down to zero by December 2024 by transferring its entries into the other two. The May-2025 list (52,958) splits 40,044 hospital/morgue + 12,914 online relative submissions — roughly a quarter of the named toll arrived through the no-body channel.

A recovered body is not required. The portal “has included since inception an option to report deaths of those buried under the rubble” (PN158). What is required is identification plus a death determination: a judicial committee of three judges with the prosecution hears the reporter and two witnesses who are not first-degree relatives. The family channel is error-prone — in the Oct 2024 – Mar 2025 revision, “almost 90% of the removed entries came from the family reporting system.”

What the list excludes. The open “missing / under the rubble, not individually reported” pool is not on it. The MoH “has never provided a count of the missing”; only the Government Media Office (which Epstein deems unreliable) publishes missing-persons claims — 7,000 (Dec 2023–Apr 2024) rising to a peak of 14,222 (18 Jan 2025), then ~10,000 (May 2025). These are a standalone estimate, not part of the named roster, though the pools partly overlap as families later register the long-missing through the portal.

The 6,187, reconciled. The figure AOAV circulated (29 Aug 2024) as deaths “confirmed via the online form” is simply the MoH's relative-reports cumulative on 6 Aug 2024 (Epstein Fig 11b). This project's own the MoH named list reproduces it: counting persons recorded only via the relative/judicial channel (no hospital tag) gives 6,093 by List 5 and 6,818 by List 6 — bracketing the figure (chart 3).

What is structurally absent. The often-cited body-disposition split (≈55% passed through a hospital / 22% buried without one / 23% missing or under rubble) is online-form intake metadata, not published in any names roster — not in this dataset, not in Epstein's tabulations. It is also a different axis from the channel tags: a body can have physically reached a hospital yet be recorded only via the portal (the e-Hospital system having collapsed after the November 2023 hospital raids), so “relative-report channel” is not the same as “no body recovered.”
