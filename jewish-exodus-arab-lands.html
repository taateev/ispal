Just before 1948, some 858,600 Jews lived across the Arab world and Turkey. Within a generation almost all were gone. This page leads with the census figures and then records, in order, the laws, pogroms, and organised departures that moved them — each entry tagged by its evidentiary source. The drivers were not uniform: a state pogrom in Iraq, a denaturalisation law, a decolonisation pull toward France in Algeria, a religious-traditional airlift from Yemen, a four-decade emigration ban in Syria. The log keeps them distinct rather than flattening them into one cause.

Population & the documented record, by year

A gentle rise to the ~858,600 peak of 1948, then an order-of-magnitude collapse over a single generation. The pre-1948 pogroms — the Farhud (1941), Tripoli (1945) — shook the communities and grew the Zionist underground, but did not yet move the numbers: populations held or grew until the post-1948 cascade. The steepest fall is inside the 1948–1967 exodus band — Iraq and Yemen emptied by 1951, Algeria's community left for France in 1962, the rest drained gradually. Syria, held by a 44-year emigration ban, is the late tail (1992). Turkey, not an Arab land, is the one community that did not collapse.

Scope & provenance. Each row is tagged: corpus = sourced from a book in the project corpus; established = a well-attested dated event; external = sourced outside the corpus — chiefly the present-day remnant figures, from DellaPergola's World Jewish Population 2023 (Morocco, Tunisia, Turkey, Algeria) and recent reporting (France 24, Times of Israel, Asharq Al-Awsat, Jerusalem Post, 2021–2024) for the near-extinct Iraq, Egypt, Syria, Yemen and Libya communities. Reading caveats: the popular Egypt "before" of 100,000 runs high (corpus 67,000–80,000); Tunisia's ~105,000 does not fit the census series (74,000 used); Syria's small Israel-immigration badly understates its disappearance (emigration was banned until 1992); Libya's main exodus was 1949–51, not 1967; Algeria's Jews went to France, ~13:1 over Israel. "Ethnic cleansing" is deliberately not used as the organising frame — the entries are census counts and dated events, and readers draw the characterisation.
