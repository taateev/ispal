The land regime · 1831–1947

The land regime as a derivation graph — 53 dated statutes, translations, registers, commission reports, transactions and fatwas, joined by 66 documented-citation edges across six actor tracks. The 1858 Ottoman Land Code emerges as the hub and single point of failure of the whole system; the deepest chain runs through transactions and reactions, not legal doctrine. With the full statute-level event log and the 1945 ownership anchor — by group, by land type, and in reverse to 1858.

The host order · 1831–1948

The sovereign-side substrate ledger: how legally frozen ground came unsealed, and why neither sovereign could hold its own line. 34 dated instruments across six lanes — civil status, land tenure, entry, the consular enforcement layer, sovereign capacity, and sovereignty itself — from the Egyptian occupation and the 1838 consulate through the Tanzimat triad (1856 status, 1858 tenure, 1867 foreign capital), the Hamidian cap and its leakage, the warden's death at San Remo, and the British successor's full arc: charter, machinery, zigzag, political cap, navy-enforced seal, exhaustion, and the second withdrawal without a successor.
