NotesFootnotes

  1. PCPSR fields several distinct questions that get loosely lumped as “armed resistance.” Shown here separately, each with the national aggregate and the West Bank / Gaza split, with verbatim wording in each caption. Support for attacks is asked by target — and the gradient is large: at the 2002–04 peak ~90% backed attacks on soldiers and settlers in the occupied territories, but only ~50% backed attacks on civilians inside Israel — a 40-point gap the single phrase “armed resistance” erases. Two further measures are different constructs: a 2001–05 efficacy belief (did the violence win rights?) and a 2010– prospective “return to armed attacks” question. Treating these as one trend would be a category error. The final three panels (1g–1i) unpack this one region at a time — overlaying the civilian-target series against the general item with the gap between them shaded — so the Gaza / West Bank divergence the national average hides is read directly off a single chart. ↩
  2. National only. PCPSR does not publish a consistent West Bank / Gaza split for the two-state and one-state support questions (only a few .sav-era regional points exist), so this subsection is not broken out by region. Two independent support questions — they do not sum to 100. Hollow rings = .sav microdata (2010–11). ↩
  3. National only · 2000–2008, then discontinued. This is a package-deal item — PCPSR item #5 in a battery of reciprocal “reconciliation steps after a final peace agreement and a Palestinian state recognized by Israel” (siblings: open borders · joint economic institutions · joint political institutions / confederation · legal measures against incitement). It asks support/oppose for a curriculum that both recognizes Israel and teaches children not to demand the return of all of Palestine. So the ~85% opposition cannot be read as opposition to recognition alone — it bundles recognition with renouncing the maximal right-of-return claim. All 12 points are .sav microdata (hollow rings); support + oppose + no-opinion sum to ~100 (“% of all respondents”). PCPSR stopped fielding the battery after Aug 2008, so there is no post-2008 continuation. ↩
  4. Each party shown with the national aggregate and the West Bank / Gaza split. Hamas runs consistently higher in Gaza than the West Bank. Fatah's regional split is press-era only (2012+); its national line runs the full 2000–2025. Both sit well below 50% — the modal answer is often ‘none / undecided’. ↩
  5. Desire to emigrate runs far higher in Gaza than the West Bank throughout; perceived PA corruption has sat at 65–87% for two decades. Emigration splits the full series; the corruption regional split is .sav-era only (2000–11; hollow rings) while its national line extends to 2023. (Safety/security extracted but volatile — off-chart.) ↩

MethodologyHow these series are built — and what they leave out

Source & convention. All figures are PCPSR. The .sav-era values (2000–2011) are computed from the released microdata — weighted (wt3 where present), “don't know” kept in the base, matching PCPSR's reported “% of all respondents.” Press-era values (2012–2025) are read from PCPSR's own press releases. Hollow ring markers flag .sav-microdata points; faint dashed lines are linear (OLS) trends; the slope tables report pp/year and the correlation r.

Regional coverage. Armed resistance, Hamas support and emigration split cleanly National / West Bank / Gaza across the whole series. Fatah's regional split is press-era only (2012+); PA-corruption's regional split is .sav-era only (2000–11); the two-state / one-state questions have no consistent regional split published, so resolution is shown national-only.

Other gaps are real, not hidden. The general “return to armed attacks” measure has a 2005–09 question-hiatus (the line interpolates it); two-state has no clean total in the 2016–17 four-option polls; press-era corruption is hand-verified (auto-extraction mis-grabbed several polls, dropped); economic-conditions was too inconsistently worded to chart; the safety/security series is extracted but volatile and held off-chart.
